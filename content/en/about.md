# About me

After graduating in 2019, I joined the game industry as an indie game artist. I spent quite some time practicing my hand-drawing skills. Soon I found that I tended to fill in the gaps within the team. For instance, when I created user interfaces, I realized that there was a gap between game design documents and UI art—a gap that a UX designer should fill. If I wanted a better result, I had to fill these gaps. Sometimes, that also meant creating key art for marketing, custom emojis for the player community, or even 3D level art.

I had a lot of confusion because everybody kept telling me that I was not "specialized" enough and that I couldn't find my "niche." So in 2022, I applied for a job at a commercial mobile game company. I spent three months there, but it didn't feel very "human"—I felt like a "tool," as did everyone else in that room. Then, for various reasons, I joined a single-player game publisher. I wore many hats there: visual designer, community manager, and marketer. I witnessed the whole launch process of some pretty cool games. I took every responsibility very seriously, and I gained recognition from both the team and our partners. I found out that I actually had the drive and execution to push things forward. It was still weird because my original anxiety was that I wasn't "specialized" enough, yet wearing a lot of hats and being helpful gave me a genuine sense of achievement and happiness. I was still struggling with the concept of being "professional," though.

In 2025, I came back to school and joined a game design master's program in Sweden. The campus is on an island called Gotland, a 40-minute boat ride from the mainland. I met many friends there, and we played many games together: D&D, _Apex Legends_, _Baldur's Gate 3_, and more. We also made many games. We all got the chance to think as game designers, considering what experience we wanted to create, what games could achieve as a medium, what messages games could deliver, and most importantly, what we, as designers, wanted to express. Among the projects I worked on were a [game](https://tianyun-yang.itch.io/lost-in-the-subway-v2) that tried to help players reclaim their sense of agency through a cathartic experience, a [game](https://ventiwang.itch.io/incubator) that used academic plagiarism as a metaphor for social inequality, a board game that made players feel uncomfortable by pointing fingers at each other, and an [interactive poem](https://tianyun-yang.itch.io/the-hole-on-the-road) that I made by myself in only one week. In the poem, I tried to portray a person who was so exhausted from work that a void manifested in the middle of their path, until they couldn't ignore it anymore and jumped into it to explore.

Before Gotland, my mind was obsessed with the idea of "finding my position in the market." But then I realized that what I really gained was a chance to be a creator, which helped me get closer to myself.

I also realized that doing all those "extra tasks" didn't mean I was "unprofessional." Instead, I did those things (which I didn't hate and was willing to do) because I had the professional responsibility to get things done. Certain parts of this industry want a hyper-specialized expert who only does one thing, but unfortunately, I just didn't choose that path.

I still want to express more as a creative person, so I'm continuing to make small games to explore and communicate.

Although a portfolio website is supposed to be "flawless" and show every perfect angle of me, I still want to cut out the superficial fluff written under the standard "STAR" framework. If you want to know what I did and what skills I have, you are very welcome to check my resume.

# About the Site

I have always been interested in personal websites; it feels like owning a little house in the cyber world. Although it seems to be of little help for landing a job in the current Chinese market, I find myself having immense enthusiasm for building this digital space from the ground up. I have to thank Codex for helping with the coding part.

I use Obsidian to manage the content of this site, and the process is a bit like a game designer filling out a spreadsheet: I make changes locally and push them through GitHub. Although I often misspell image links, which leads to bugs, I am still a little proud of myself for building this (partially) sustainable way of managing the site.

This raw cyber shell is still a work in progress, but either way, you are welcome to take a seat and have a warm electronic beverage..
